A Blockchain-Enabled Framework for Misinformation Monitoring

Multilingual AI triage and a shared, tamper-evident ledger for South Africa's election fact-checkers

Introduction

In South Africa's 2024 election, AI deepfakes spread through politically active WhatsApp groups, and the fact-checks posted in reply were often dismissed as propaganda.

Fact-checking works, but each organisation keeps its own record on its own rating scale. Detection tools rarely cover African languages: 67% of studies use English data and under 10% target low-resource languages.

Research question
How can an integrated AI and blockchain framework enable tamper-resistant, transparent, multilingual monitoring and verification of election misinformation in South Africa?

Methodology

A four-stage pipeline
1
Ingest: claims enter an Apache Kafka stream that absorbs election-day surges.
2
Triage: AfroXLMR-large-76L (LoRA) flags likely misinformation with a confidence score.
3
Verify: every organisation except the submitter reviews; ⌊E/2⌋ + 1 matching votes finalise automatically, a split is DISPUTED.
4
Record: Hyperledger Fabric stores a SHA-256 hash and metadata; claim text stays off-chain in IPFS.
A synthetic multilingual corpus

No labelled election corpus existed in Sepedi or isiZulu. 5,000 scripted prompts asked Gemini 3.6 Flash for paired true and false statements, which were then machine-translated.

Why deploy LoRA?
Two of three full fine-tuning runs ended with validation loss 0.40–0.49 above training loss; every LoRA run stayed within 0.04, while training only 1.29% of parameters.

System architecture

A permissioned Hyperledger Fabric consortium with Raft ordering: no fees, deterministic finality, and roles enforced inside chaincode from X.509 certificate attributes.

Conclusion

An Afrocentric encoder trained on translated synthetic data detects misinformation in Sepedi and isiZulu close to English. A Fabric consortium records joint verdicts tamper-evidently with no personal data on-chain; commitment stays stable while final verdicts slow as the required majority grows.

Limitations and next steps

Synthetic inputs, scripted reviews and one host. Next: real election posts, a multi-host deployment, a fact-checker web interface and per-user identities.
