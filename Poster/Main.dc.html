1. Introduction

In South Africa's 2024 election, AI deepfakes spread through WhatsApp groups and fact-checks were dismissed as propaganda [1]. Fact-checkers keep separate records, and detection tools rarely cover African languages:

67%
of detection studies use English data [2]
<10%
target low-resource languages [2]
Research question
How can an integrated AI and blockchain framework enable tamper-resistant, transparent, multilingual monitoring and verification of election misinformation in South Africa?

3. System architecture

A permissioned Hyperledger Fabric consortium [5] with Raft ordering: no fees, deterministic finality, and roles enforced inside chaincode from X.509 certificate attributes.

3.1 Roles enforced in chaincode
Official
submits, expires, admits members
Fact-checker
submits reports and fact-checks

read-only audit

4. Performance of deployed model

Deployed detector: AfroXLMR-large-76L [3] with LoRA [4]. Means of three seeds, 1,393 test statements.

0.882
F1 on the misinformation class, ± 0.015 across seeds; AUC 0.940
9 in 10 false claims are flagged for fact-checkers (recall 0.902), and 86% of flagged claims really are false (precision 0.862).
4.2 Why LoRA, not full fine-tuning
98%
of full fine-tuning's F1 (0.882 vs 0.899)
1.29%
of weights trained: a 7.3 M adapter on a shared base
≤ 0.04
loss gap at stop; 2 of 3 full runs overfit (0.40–0.49)
Full fine-tuning overfits synthetic data; LoRA should generalise better.
What this means for the framework
The model triages; it does not decide. Its label and confidence are stored on-chain beside every vote, and the consortium (section 5) makes the final call, so model errors stay visible and can be outvoted.
