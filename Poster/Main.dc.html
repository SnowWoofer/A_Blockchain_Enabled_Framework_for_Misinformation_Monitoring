A Blockchain-Enabled Framework for Misinformation Monitoring

Multilingual AI triage and a shared, tamper-evident ledger for South Africa's election fact-checkers

1. Introduction

In South Africa's 2024 election, AI deepfakes spread through WhatsApp groups and fact-checks were dismissed as propaganda [1]. Fact-checkers keep separate records, and detection tools rarely cover African languages:

67%
of detection studies use English data [2]
<10%
target low-resource languages [2]
Research question
How can an integrated AI and blockchain framework enable tamper-resistant, transparent, multilingual monitoring and verification of election misinformation in South Africa?

2. Methodology

2.1 A four-stage pipeline
1
Ingest: an Apache Kafka stream absorbs election-day surges.
2
Triage: AfroXLMR-large-76L [3] with LoRA [4] scores each claim.
3
Verify: every other organisation votes; ⌊E/2⌋ + 1 matching votes finalise, otherwise DISPUTED.
4
Record: Hyperledger Fabric [5] stores a SHA-256 hash; claim text stays in IPFS.
2.2 A synthetic multilingual corpus

No Sepedi or isiZulu election corpus existed, so Gemini 3.6 Flash wrote paired true and false statements, machine-translated from English.

3. System architecture

A permissioned Hyperledger Fabric consortium [5] with Raft ordering: no fees, deterministic finality, and roles enforced inside chaincode from X.509 certificate attributes.

3.1 Roles enforced in chaincode
Official
submits, expires, admits members
Fact-checker
submits reports and fact-checks
Observer
read-only audit
3.2 POPIA by design
s 10
Only hash, CID, AI metadata and org ID on-chain.
s 13
Fixed schema; no free-form personal fields.
s 14
Claim text off-chain in IPFS, so deletable.
s 19
SHA-256, TLS channels and chaincode RBAC.

Why deploy LoRA?
Two of three full fine-tuning runs overfitted (validation loss 0.40–0.49 above training loss); every LoRA run stayed within 0.04 while training only 1.29% of parameters.

6. Conclusion

An Afrocentric encoder trained on translated synthetic data detects misinformation in Sepedi and isiZulu close to English; a Fabric consortium records joint verdicts tamper-evidently.

Next steps: real election posts, a multi-host deployment, a stakeholder web interface and per-user identities.
